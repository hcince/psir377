    About the course & learning outcomes

    This course introduces the origins, dynamics, and consequences of social and political movements from theoretical and historical perspectives. Major approaches and theories of reform, revolution, and social movements are covered, alongside historically important cases and contemporary mobilization at local, national, and global levels.

    1. Conceive the complex basis of mass movements.
    2. Discern significant differences and similarities between the social and the political.
    3. Understand the significance of agency and leadership in mass movements.
    4. Gain insight into the relationship between state, civil society, and the individual.
    5. Understand movements in a broader global context.
    6. Develop complex argumentation skills in speech and writing.

    05 / FROM THEORY TO CASE

    Your project, in context

    Group project · 20%
    In-class presentations · 25 December

    Choose a significant social or political movement, then use a theoretical framework discussed in class to analyze its social and political character.

    1. 01

      Choose a movement

      A historical or contemporary case, local, national, and/or global in scope.

    2. 02

      Establish the context

      Explain the movement’s sociopolitical and historical setting.

    3. 03

      Select a framework

      Use a specific theoretical framework covered in class discussion.

    4. 04

      Build the analysis

      Discuss the movement’s social and political character through that framework.

    5. 05

      Present in class

      Bring your group’s analysis to the classroom on 25 December.
